# The story: six slides, one sentence

The buyer should be able to repeat the deck in one sentence after one pass. Write that sentence first, in the
buyer's language, and check every slide against it:

> "Some of my small depositors are big players elsewhere, I can't see them, and Addressable can show me this month."

If a slide does not make that sentence more believable or more urgent, it is not in the deck.

## The arc

| # | Slide | Its one job | The buyer thinks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `cover` | Name the blind spot, as a claim about them | "Is that true of us?" |
| 2 | `why_now` | Make this month different from last quarter | "This is timely." |
| 3 | `hero` | The one number they cannot compute themselves | "I didn't know that." |
| 4 | `highlights` | Two or three facts that make the number matter | "This touches my targets." |
| 5 | `paths` | What they can do with it, their path marked | "That's a thing I could start." |
| 6 | `next` | One small ask and one person | "I can say yes to that." |
| + | `method` | How the numbers were made, for the analyst they forward it to | "This will survive scrutiny." |

Why-now comes before the hero when the event is strong and near the prospect (a rival's outage last week). When the
urgency is a trend in our own data, put the hero first and the why-now after it, so the trend lands on a number the
buyer already cares about. Never open with Addressable: no "about us", no product slide, no logo wall. The method
slide and the close are where we appear.

## Choosing the hero and the highlights

List every candidate fact from the data, then score each 0 to 2 on three questions and keep the top one as the hero
and the next two or three as highlights:

- **New to them?** Could they get it from their own back office or a public dashboard? If yes, 0. (Their own top-1%
  share of deposits fails this test: they see it daily. What their users do *elsewhere* passes.)
- **About their number?** Does it move what this buyer is measured on (see `buyer-review.md`, role lenses)?
- **Actionable this quarter?** Is there something they could do about it with one of the two paths?

A fact that scores 2, 2, 2 is the hero. A fact that is merely large is not. "$826M deposited at these casinos" is
big and useless; "1,296 players deposit under $500 at one casino and over $5,000 at others" is small and is the
pitch. Prefer a count of people the buyer could act on over a sum of money, and a share ("30%") over a raw count
when the base is not the prospect's own.

Highlights should be three different kinds of fact, not three sizes of the same one: for example a share (how
common), a concentration (how much it matters) and a magnitude (how big per person).

## Headlines

- The headline is the conclusion, not the topic: "What one casino can't see about its players", not "Player overlap".
- Ten words at most. One `*emphasised phrase*` on the words that carry the point.
- Second person, their vocabulary (the vertical's words: players, bettors, traders, clients).
- Read the headlines alone, in order. They should be the argument. If they are not, fix the headlines before
  touching anything else.

## Words

- Plain words, short sentences, no adjectives doing the work of numbers. No hype (the lint lists the words).
- Round to what a person can say aloud: "1,296", "30%", "$11.6K". Never more precise than the source, never a
  bigger round number than the truth. A share needs its base in the `basis` line.
- Say what was measured: "fund two or more casinos", not "are disloyal". Deposits are deposits: not spend, wagering
  or revenue.
- Every count has its base and every change its baseline, in the line or the `basis` ("1,296 of 133,000", "1 in 5,
  against 1 in 10 normally"). When the base is not the prospect's own, lead with the share ("1 in 100").
- Each number once. If it is the hero, it is not also a highlight.
- Anything you want to add goes in `notes`: the talk track is where nuance lives. The slide stays at one idea.

## Before and after

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Addressable's cross-platform identity graph leverages on-chain and off-chain signals to unlock hidden value" | "1,296 players deposit under $500 at one casino and over $5,000 at others." |
| A table of 12 competitors with deposits per casino | "30% play at two or more casinos" (the table goes to the call) |
| "Recent market events create a unique opportunity" | "22 Oct: Rivalbet pauses withdrawals for 3 days. In the two weeks after, 1 in 5 of its depositors funded a different casino." |
| "Contact us to learn more" | "Share a user sample. We show you the hidden VIPs." |

## When there are more facts than room

That is the normal case. Options, in order: cut; move to `notes`; move to the method slide; make a second deck for
the second meeting. Never shrink type, add a slide past the budget, or put two ideas on one slide.
